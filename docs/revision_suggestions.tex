\section*{Scope and Inputs}

This review opens a new revision cycle. It converts the AAAI-27 Phase-1
reviews of Submission \#3464 (Reviewer 8sNa, Rating 3 / Confidence 3;
Reviewer mMVb, Rating 3 / Confidence 5; the AAAI AI Review) into concrete
actions, and it assesses the manuscript against the IEEE Transactions on
Parallel and Distributed Systems (TPDS) standard.

Author decisions that fix the direction of this cycle:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Target venue: IEEE TPDS (regular paper, IEEEtran journal format).
  \item Narrative: a negative-result and diagnostic characterization study.
        The paper no longer claims that activation entropy replaces offline
        profiling. It asks what determines the optimal chunk of chunked
        linear-recurrence kernels, and shows with causal evidence why an
        activation-statistic scheduler cannot beat a tuned static or
        shape-keyed policy.
  \item Supplementary experiments in scope: the core evidence chain
        (per-call oracle variation, feature-to-oracle prediction, strongest
        static baseline, break-even amortization), Mamba-2 and Gated
        DeltaNet coverage, and an auto-tuning baseline. A full batched
        serving system is out of scope; batch size is covered as a
        kernel-level sweep dimension only.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{Overall Assessment}

The reviewers agree that the paper studies a relevant systems problem and
that its end-to-end measurements are honest. They also agree that the
contribution, as framed, is not supported: the headline kernel gains are
relative to a weak Static-64 baseline and are reproduced exactly by
Static-512, no experiment shows that entropy predicts a changing per-call
optimum, the evaluated scheduler is inconsistent with the defined rule, and
the architecture coverage stops at Mamba-1.

An internal audit made for this plan found a more serious problem with
the kernel-level evidence. The chunk sweeps behind the consumer-GPU,
server-GPU, H800 and perturbation tables split the sequence into
independent calls of a stock selective-scan kernel. No recurrent state is
carried across the split points, so the computed scan is not the model's
scan, and the measured speedups mostly count fewer Python-level launches.
The ``isolated kernel latency'' column of the kernel-summary table comes
from an element-wise placeholder kernel, not from a scan. These results
cannot support any claim about chunk sensitivity of a correct scan kernel.
The only recurrence-preserving chunk measurements in the paper are the
routed ablation (a patched live kernel), where all fixed chunks from 128 to
2048 lie within 2.6\% of each other.

As a TPDS paper, a characterization study is in scope, provided it is
methodologically clean, covers the relevant kernel family, and yields
actionable guidance: what drives the optimal chunk, how large the
opportunity is, and when runtime adaptation can repay its cost.

\section*{Required Revisions}

\subsection*{A. Framing and Claims}

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{A\arabic*}]
  \item \textbf{Reframe the paper as a negative-result characterization.}
        Rewrite the title, abstract, introduction, contributions and
        conclusion. The central question is whether per-call activation
        statistics carry scheduling information for chunked
        linear-recurrence kernels beyond shape, sequence length and
        hardware. Remove the ``replace offline profiling'' claim and the
        concept-and-feasibility framing. Answer reviewer questions Q1 and Q5
        explicitly: state which deployment scenario, if any, could benefit
        from runtime adaptation, using the break-even analysis (C5).
  \item \textbf{Remove the invalid kernel-level evidence.} Delete the
        $4.41\times$ and $3.55\times$ headline numbers and the tables built
        on the chunk-splitting harness (kernel summary, multi-platform
        three-policy table, perturbation sweep, H800 W1 supplement). Replace
        them with recurrence-preserving measurements (C1, C2). State
        briefly, in the evaluation methodology, why split-call timing is not
        a valid chunk measurement, so readers of the earlier preprint
        understand the change.
  \item \textbf{Make the evaluated scheduler match the defined method.}
        Eq.~(2) clips at $C_{\max}=512$, but the routed ablation reports
        entropy and proxy policies at chunks 1024 and 2048. Either define the
        routed variant (its chunk range and mapping) in the method section
        or rerun it with the defined rule. Every scheduler row must be traceable
        to one written definition.
  \item \textbf{Report comparisons against the strongest static baseline
        only.} All speedup or regret figures are relative to the per-setting
        best static chunk (and to the auto-tuned configuration, C4), never to
        an arbitrary chunk.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{B. Method, Theory and Presentation}

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{B\arabic*}]
  \item \textbf{Correct the bin-count claim.} Table~1 shows $H/\log K$
        changing from 0.763 to 0.881 as $K$ grows; the chunk stays at 512
        only because every point falls in the saturated output bucket. Replace
        ``invariance'' with ``decision saturation'' and state the actual
        dependence on $K$.
  \item \textbf{Fix Eq.~(1).} With $\epsilon>0$ the normalized entropy is not
        exactly in $[0,1]$ and the uniform value is not exactly $\log K$.
        Use the convention $0\log 0=0$, or state the bounds with $\epsilon$.
  \item \textbf{Fix or remove Theorem~2.} Its strictness condition is
        incomplete: a non-permutation doubly-stochastic matrix can leave a
        non-uniform vector unchanged. Given that the Hadamard analysis is
        empirically falsified and does not support the scheduler, move it,
        together with the fusion-depth prototype and Theorem~1, out of the
        main text.
  \item \textbf{Add a causal account of chunk cost.} Answer reviewer Q3
        with an argument in the main text: for a fixed shape, data type and
        hardware, a dense chunked scan executes a data-independent
        instruction and memory-access stream, so its latency, and therefore
        the optimal chunk, is a function of shape and hardware, not of
        activation values. Entropy can only correlate with the optimum
        through its correlation with shape or sequence length. Support the
        argument empirically (C1).
  \item \textbf{Organize the evidence in causal order}: static chunk
        sensitivity $\rightarrow$ value invariance $\rightarrow$ variation of
        the per-call oracle $\rightarrow$ feature-to-oracle prediction
        $\rightarrow$ routed decision cost $\rightarrow$ output preservation
        $\rightarrow$ amortization.
  \item \textbf{Add a system-path schematic} before the experiments. It
        should show where entropy collection, synchronization, dispatch and
        scan execution happen, and which cost each experiment tier includes.
  \item \textbf{Separate microbenchmarks from deployment measurements}
        consistently in tables, captions and text.
  \item \textbf{Scope the quality claim.} The LongBench study shows identical
        greedy tokens and zero metric deltas, not bitwise-equal hidden states
        or logits. Rephrase ``bitwise identical'' and ``recurrence-preserving''
        accordingly.
  \item \textbf{Fix internal inconsistencies}: the mixed-regime section says
        160 prompts with 20 per regime while its table reports 8 samples per
        regime; the kernel-summary latency ratio differs from its speedup
        column; the calibration setting ($H_{\text{ref}}=8.0$ versus
        $\log K$) must be stated for every reported result.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{C. Experiments}

All new timing experiments use recurrence-preserving kernels in which the
chunk is a real tiling parameter, report mean, standard deviation and a
95\% confidence interval over repeated timed runs after warmup, and are
run on at least two GPU generations (a consumer GPU and an RTX PRO 6000
Blackwell server GPU).

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{C\arabic*}]
  \item \textbf{Value invariance of kernel latency.} For fixed shape, data
        type and chunk, time the scan on inputs with very different
        entropy (uniform, Gaussian, Laplace, sparse at several densities,
        real captured activations). Cover the Mamba-1 selective scan, the
        Mamba-2 SSD chunked scan and the Gated DeltaNet chunked kernel. The
        expected outcome is that latency differences across inputs stay
        within measurement noise while differences across chunks do not.
  \item \textbf{Per-call oracle variation on real traces.} Capture the scan
        inputs of real Mamba-1 and Mamba-2 checkpoints over a diverse prompt
        set (the mixed-regime corpus plus long-context prompts, sequence
        lengths from tens to several thousand tokens). For each captured call
        and each candidate chunk, measure latency and record the per-call
        oracle chunk, the regret of every static chunk, and the spread of
        entropy. Include batch size $\{1,4,16\}$ as a sweep dimension.
  \item \textbf{Feature-to-oracle prediction.} Using C2, compare predictors
        of the oracle chunk: global static, sequence length only, shape
        (sequence length, batch, width, layer), entropy only, other activation
        moments, and all features combined. Report accuracy and mean and
        tail regret relative to the per-call oracle under held-out
        evaluation. This answers reviewer Q2 directly.
  \item \textbf{Auto-tuning baseline.} Add a standard offline auto-tuner
        (a Triton-style autotune over chunk and launch configuration, keyed
        on shape), report its tuning cost, and compare its latency with the
        best static chunk and with the entropy scheduler under identical
        conditions. This answers reviewer Q4.
  \item \textbf{Break-even amortization.} Combine the measured one-time
        profiling or tuning cost, the per-call runtime scheduling overhead,
        and the attainable per-call gain from C2--C3 into a break-even model
        (number of calls or requests after which each policy pays off).
        State the conditions under which runtime adaptation could win.
  \item \textbf{Architecture coverage.} Report chunk sensitivity and
        oracle behavior for Mamba-2 (SSD) and Gated DeltaNet, positioned
        against the arithmetic-intensity analysis of Tiled Flash Linear
        Attention.
  \item \textbf{Mechanism visualization.} Add figures for the entropy
        distribution across workloads, latency versus chunk curves per shape,
        entropy versus oracle chunk, and the regret of each policy
        (reviewer mMVb weakness 6).
  \item \textbf{Statistical resolution of existing results.} For the routed
        ablation and the mixed-regime table, report confidence intervals and
        avoid claiming a unique oracle where competing chunks are within
        noise (Static-512 versus Static-128 differ by 1.20\,ms against
        standard deviations of 10--12\,ms). Remove the TPU and Tesla rows
        that contain no sweep, or label them as single-point measurements.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{D. Related Work and Citations}

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{D\arabic*}]
