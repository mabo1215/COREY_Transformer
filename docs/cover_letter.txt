Dear Editor-in-Chief,

We are pleased to submit our manuscript entitled "Shape Sets the Chunk: A Compile-Informed Cost Model and a No-Gain Bound for Data-Dependent Dispatch of Chunked Linear-Recurrence Kernels" for consideration as a regular paper in the ACM Transactions on Architecture and Code Optimization (TACO). It is a new paper, not an extension of a conference paper.

The chunked linear-recurrence kernels behind Mamba, Mamba-2 and Gated DeltaNet expose a chunk size that trades on-chip reuse and occupancy against sequential and per-chunk work. Libraries fix it per kernel, and tuning it per shape scales poorly when batching, chunked prefill, tensor-parallel sharding and mixed GPU types multiply the shapes a kernel sees. The manuscript asks how this code-optimization parameter should be chosen: predicted from the shape and the compiled kernel, or selected per call from the data the kernel receives.

The main contributions of the manuscript are:

1. A characterization of where the chunk matters. In eager execution the library defaults suffice for Mamba-2 SSD, Gated DeltaNet and the stock dispatch of the reference Mamba-1 CUDA scan, but under CUDA graphs the SSD and Gated DeltaNet defaults lose 16 to 22% and a shape-keyed table is needed; on a Triton Mamba-1 scan the chunk moves latency by up to 8.5 times.

2. A compile-informed cost model that reads the register allocation, spills and tile layout of the compiled kernel and is calibrated once per GPU with at most eight parameters. Without timing the target shapes it selects the chunk of 2360 captured prefill calls with 1.3 to 7.3% mean regret on three GPUs (3.5% on a data-centre GPU measured after its terms were frozen) and needs no retuning at tensor-parallel shard widths, where a table tuned at the unsharded width loses up to 65%. We also report where it does not hold: on an independent public kernel, on the reference scan at a fixed thread count and on the production kernels under CUDA graphs.

3. A no-gain bound for data-dependent dispatch: at any decision cost, a policy that reads the activations gains at most the oracle headroom of a shape-keyed table. We instantiate it with value-invariance experiments with placement controls, predictor and information analyses (activation statistics add nothing to the shape) and noise-corrected headroom estimates, and show that on the Mamba-1 scans from 370M to 2.8B parameters the headroom over an exact-shape table is below one host synchronization at every tuned shape.

4. Deployment guidance: the chunk effect in milliseconds per forward pass, the break-even of online policies and of the cost model against profiling, a replay of public LLM serving traces (Azure LLM inference traces and BurstGPT) that shows how the choice of shape key decides whether any online policy can pay off, a deployment recipe for tuning caches, and a measurement pitfall (split-call chunk timing) that does not measure a chunked kernel.

We believe the manuscript fits TACO because it concerns code optimization and performance modeling of GPU kernels: a cost model built from compiler output (registers, spills, tile layout and compiler specializations), auto-tuning and tuning-cache design, and the limits of input-aware variant selection for dense kernels, evaluated on three GPU generations (Ampere, Hopper and Blackwell).

An earlier version of this study, with different conclusions, is publicly available as a preprint on arXiv (arXiv:2604.10597); it has not been published in any journal or conference proceedings. The preprint proposed an activation-entropy-driven chunk scheduler, whereas this manuscript shows that activation statistics carry no information about the optimal chunk beyond the shape and that shape-keyed tuning, or a compile-informed cost model, is the appropriate mechanism. The kernel-level chunk results of the preprint were obtained with a split-call timing harness, which cuts the sequence into independent kernel calls and mostly measures the number of launches; this manuscript withdraws all such results and replaces them with measurements that change the chunk inside a single recurrence-preserving launch. To preserve double-anonymous review, the manuscript describes the pitfall in general terms and does not refer to the preprint.

This manuscript is original; apart from the preprint noted above, it has not been published previously, and it is not under consideration for publication elsewhere. All authors have approved the submission, and there are no conflicts of interest that would affect the review process. In compliance with the double-anonymous review policy of TACO, the manuscript and the Supplementary Material are anonymized, and the code and data are provided as a separate anonymized supplementary archive. The use of AI tools (Grammarly for grammar checking; Claude for debugging experiment code, writing and debugging parts of the measurement and analysis scripts, and cross-checking reported numbers) is disclosed in the manuscript, following the ACM Policy on Authorship.

Thank you for considering our submission. We look forward to receiving the reviewers' comments.
