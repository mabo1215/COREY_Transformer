\section*{Scope and Inputs}

This file opens revision cycle taco-r1. It synthesizes a fresh, independent
five-reviewer panel (Editor-in-Chief, R1 methodology, R2 domain, R3
production-inference perspective, devil's advocate) that reviewed the current
manuscript (ACM acmsmall, main article 24 pages including references;
Supplementary Material 48 pages) jointly, from the sources, the compiled PDFs
and the result files only, without reference to earlier review rounds, against
the standard of ACM Transactions on Architecture and Code Optimization (new
paper; initial submissions limited to 20 pages excluding references;
double-anonymous review). The full reports are in
\texttt{docs/review\_reports/taco\_r1\_*.md}; each concern below cites the
findings it merges (EIC E$n$, R1 M$n$, R2 D$n$, R3 P$n$, DA$n$).

Effort classes: (a) text only; (b) offline analysis of existing result files;
(c) new GPU measurement, with an estimate in GPU-hours. Items of class (c) are
run only after the author approves the hosts and budget.

\section*{Editorial Decision}

\textbf{Major Revision.} Four reviewers recommend Major Revision (EIC
57.5/100, R1, R2 borderline reject-and-resubmit, R3); the devil's advocate
recommends Reject in the current framing and Major Revision for a reframed
version. Two devil's-advocate findings are CRITICAL (DA1, DA2), so the paper
cannot be accepted until they are resolved.

\paragraph{Consensus strengths (keep).} Recurrence-preserving timing with an
fp32 output check; shuffled rounds, confidence intervals, TOST, cluster
bootstrap and permutation tests; the value-versus-placement and warm/cold-L2
interventions; the Triton specialization-bit observation (a length not
divisible by 16 is another kernel with another optimal chunk); honest
reporting of where the model fails; the CUDA-graph result for the production
kernels. All number spot-checks (about 40 values across R1, DA and EIC)
matched the result files.

\paragraph{Consensus weaknesses.} (i) The title-level contributions are an
elementary bound tested against a policy that the paper says nobody proposes,
and a cost model demonstrated on one in-house kernel that is 1.23 times slower
than the reference scan; (ii) timing conventions and regret summaries change
from claim to claim, and the launch-inclusive headline session did not
reproduce; (iii) the production-relevant regimes (CUDA graphs, packed
variable-length calls, a data-centre GPU) are thin; (iv) the paper is very
dense and leans on a 48-page supplement.

published runtime or paper proposes value-dependent dispatch''), Secs.~3--4,
7.1--7.3, 7.6 and Contribution 3 spend about 40\% of the body refuting an
entropy-driven scheduler; Proposition 1(a) is one inequality and 1(b) is close

\section*{Acceptance Criteria}
\begin{enumerate}[label=A\arabic*.]
  \item Compliance: V1--V4 done; within 20 pages excluding references.
  \item Framing (M1): the bound is a lemma, not a title claim; the entropy
        scheduler is a control; prior art cited; title and abstract centred on
        chunk selection and tuning economics.
  \item Generality (M2): the method shown on at least two independent kernels
        with frozen form, or the model explicitly scoped as a single-kernel
        case study with claims reduced accordingly.
  \item Correctness (M3): the launch-tuple coupling disclosed and decomposed;
        Eq.~(3) matches the implementation; calibration fit documented with
        uncertainty.
  \item Statistics (M4): one primary convention; latency-weighted figures
        next to per-call ones; a priori model selection with paired CIs.
  \item Economics (M5): fair top-$k$ and budget-matched baselines; a cost
        comparison against profiling every key.
  \item Production regime (M6): graph-mode evidence on a data-centre GPU and
        packed-call timing, or the corresponding claims scoped to the single
        GPU and call type measured.
  \item Proportionate parallel-deployment claims and a runtime-grade recipe
        (M7, M8); a readable, self-contained main article and a consistent,
        provenance-clean supplement (M9, M10).
\end{enumerate}

about 15--25 GPU-hours on a data-centre GPU plus the RTX PRO~6000 (M2a, M6a,
M6b, M6d). Full optional set: about 40--80 GPU-hours. All other items are text
