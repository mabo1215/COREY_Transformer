Dear Editor,

We are pleased to submit our manuscript entitled "Measuring and Modeling the Chunk Size of Linear-Recurrence GPU Kernels: Execution Regimes, Measurement Bias and the Limits of Tuning" for consideration as a research article in the Journal of Parallel and Distributed Computing.

The chunked linear-recurrence kernels behind Mamba, Mamba-2 and Gated DeltaNet layers expose a chunk size that trades on-chip reuse and occupancy against sequential and per-chunk work. The manuscript is a measurement and modeling study of this parameter: how large its effect is, which execution regime exposes it, what a timing can mistake for it, and when a model can replace timing. We study three production kernels (the Mamba-1 CUDA scan, the Mamba-2 SSD pipeline and the Gated DeltaNet pipeline) and a Triton scan that we wrote and use as a stress case. The measurements range from prefill calls captured from 370M to 2.8B parameter checkpoints up to whole prefill steps captured as CUDA graphs, on Ampere, Hopper and Blackwell GPUs.

The main findings of the manuscript are:

1. At the prefill-step level, tuning the chunk beyond the library default is second-order on the one GPU we measured. In a whole prefill step captured as one CUDA graph on a stock RTX 3090, a held-out shape-keyed table is equivalent to the default within +/-0.5% over steps of 256 to 8192 tokens (+/-1% for one model under stricter analyses), with a largest gain of 0.23% (95% interval 0.13 to 0.41), because the scan is only 5 to 19% of the step. A wrong static chunk, by contrast, costs up to 15%. We ran no serving engine and no data-centre GPU at this level.

2. The execution regime decides where the chunk is visible. On the 370M checkpoints, launch-inclusive latency follows the slower of the host's launches and the device. In single-call CUDA-graph replays the library defaults leave 1.0 to 5.9% of the scan time, mostly on short calls.

3. Buffer placement, and input values under a power cap, can move a single timing more than the chunk does. A tuning cache that times each key once, on one allocation or on synthetic inputs, records these effects as chunk or data effects.

4. The best chunk follows the kernel's specialization signature and the device. On captured calls, adding activation entropy to the shape key changes the held-out selection regret by at most 0.03 points, and a single compiler divisibility bit, which a length-bucket key loses, changes the compiled kernel and its best chunk.

5. A compile-informed model of the stress kernel selects with 0.8 to 7.4% regret with one calibration key left out, yet profiling every key a scheduler can enumerate takes seconds per GPU and costs less than calibrating that model. The model remains useful for keys that cannot be enumerated and for GPUs that cannot be timed.

We believe the manuscript fits the journal's interest in the performance analysis of parallel applications and in accelerator-based applications and platforms. It continues a line of work published in the journal on GPU autotuning and on analytical and learned performance prediction, which it discusses: Filipovic et al. (2022) on hardware counters in autotuning, Ernst et al. (2023) on analytical performance estimation during code generation, and Amaris et al. (2023) on analytical and machine-learning predictions of GPU kernel execution times.

Related preprint: the authors of this manuscript also wrote the preprint "COREY: Entropy-Guided Runtime Chunk Scheduling for Selective Scan Kernels" (arXiv:2604.10597, April 2026, revised May 2026), which has not been peer reviewed or published in any journal or conference proceedings. The preprint proposed a runtime scheduler that selects the chunk from the entropy of the activations. The present manuscript is a new study whose conclusions differ in part: it uses that design as the control for data-dependent dispatch. The kernel-level timing results of the preprint came from a harness that split the sequence into independent kernel calls and so mostly measured the number of launches; this manuscript replaces them with measurements that change the chunk inside a single launch that carries the recurrent state, and it states this relation to the preprint in its text.

This manuscript is original; apart from the preprint noted above, it has not been published previously, and it is not under consideration for publication elsewhere. All authors have approved the submission, and there are no conflicts of interest that would affect the review process. The code, result files and scripts behind every table and figure accompany the submission as a separate archive. The declaration of generative AI use is at the end of the manuscript.

Thank you for considering our submission. We look forward to receiving the reviewers' comments.
