\section{Verdict}
\textbf{Major revision before submission.} The measurement work is rigorous and fits
the JPDC topics of performance analysis of parallel applications and accelerator-based
applications and platforms; JPDC has also published GPU autotuning and analytical GPU
performance models. As written, the paper is still framed as a tuning recipe for one
kernel family. Its null headline is stronger than its design supports, and it leads
with the favourable end of its model evidence.

\textit{Venue requirements confirmed:} single-anonymized review; highlights of 3 to 5
bullet points, at most 85 characters each, in a separate editable file; a declaration
of generative AI use at submission.
\textit{Not confirmed; check on the Guide for Authors before upload:} any length limit
and the required title-page content. The journal's own page could not be opened from
the working environment.

\section{Findings and Actions}

\subsection*{Positioning}

\finding{S1}{The paper presents itself as tuning methodology, not as a performance study}
\evidence The abstract asks ``how a runtime should key and fill a chunk tuning cache''
and ends with ``We derive a recipe''. The title names selection and keying.\\
\action Retitle and rewrite the abstract as a measurement and modeling study. Lead the
contributions with the measurement, regime and model findings, and state what carries
over to other GPU kernels. Move the recipe under a subsection titled ``Practical
Implications''.

\subsection*{Validity of the headline claim}

\finding{C1}{``End to end'' overstates what was measured}
\evidence Contribution 1 and the Conclusion call the chunk second-order ``end to end'',
but the evidence is a replayed prefill forward pass. Limitations states that no serving
engine was run; decode and scheduling are absent.\\
\action Replace ``end to end'' with ``at the prefill-step level'' in the abstract,
contributions, claims table, results and conclusion.

\finding{C2}{Service time is not response time}
\evidence All step-level results are service times. In an M/G/1 queue the elasticity of
mean wait with respect to service time is $2+\rho/(1-\rho)$: 3 at $\rho=0.5$ and 11 at
$\rho=0.9$. Continuous batching is not M/G/1, so this shows only that the question is
open.\\
\action Run a trace-driven simulation with the measured per-shape step latencies under
the public trace arrivals. Sweep the load, report waiting and prefill response time
(mean and tail) with intervals, and state the load range in which ``second-order'' holds.

\finding{C3}{The step-level study stops where the effect is largest}
\evidence Whole-step shapes begin at $L=256$. The table gain on SSD 370M rises from 0 at
$L=8192$ to about $+2.3\%$ at $L=256$, and 6--16\% of Azure and 50--83\% of BurstGPT
prompts are shorter than 256 tokens and are represented by the 256-token step.\\
\action Add whole-step measurements at $L\in\{16,32,64,128\}$, batch 1 and 4, and
recompute the trace-weighted results from them.

\finding{C4}{The headline rests on one GPU}
\evidence The whole-step result comes from one stock RTX~3090.\\
\action Repeat the whole-step experiment on a data-centre GPU. If that is not possible,
keep the single-GPU scope in every statement of the headline and treat the data-centre
case as untested.

\finding{C5}{A null result needs an equivalence test}
\evidence Table~\texttt{tab:model\_graph} gives ranges without intervals. Per-shape
session gaps reach 2.5 points (GDN $256\times1$: $-2.39$ vs.\ $+0.15$) and 1.0 point (SSD
370M $512\times1$: $+1.98$ vs.\ $+0.94$), larger than the 0.3\% headline. The ``best gain''
column is an in-session minimum and is biased upward.\\
\action State and justify an equivalence margin. Report intervals on the
latency-weighted gain across rounds and sessions and the smallest detectable effect.

\finding{C6}{``Carry no information'' is broader than the evidence}
\evidence The claims table and contributions use unqualified wording. The evidence is
bounded and conditional: entropy changes the tree regret by at most 0.03 points (paired
intervals within $\pm0.2$) on captured calls, some inputs have another best chunk (regret
up to 5.1\%), and the H800 sweep shows a split with regret up to 5.7\%.\\
\action Report the bound rather than a universal statement, and retitle
Section~\texttt{sec:res\_values} accordingly.

\subsection*{Performance model}

\finding{M1}{The reported accuracy is the favourable end of the evidence}
\evidence The main text leads with 0.6--2.1\% from a rule ``fixed only after'' comparing
six alternatives. The supplement reports that 2208 of the 2298 calibration timings are
short timings in unrandomized sweep order (without the exclusive-GPU guard on two GPUs),
that a fit on full-protocol timings alone selects with 0.73, 4.28 and 6.05\%, and that
leave-one-key-out regret is 0.8, 7.4 and 3.7\%, which it calls the honest uncertainty. The
tables keep a three-restart fit although 50 starts reach a 16\% lower objective on the
RTX~3090.\\
\action Lead the main text with the leave-one-key-out and full-protocol results and label
the target-calibration rule as exploratory. Optional: freeze the whole procedure and test
it prospectively on a GPU not used to design it.

\finding{M2}{The model structure is not identified}
\evidence Eight parameters are fitted on twelve keys. The RTX~3090 full-protocol fit has
two optima within 0.5\% of the objective (regret 1.6 and 4.3\%). The form
$\max(A+I,\,I\,W/W_0)$ fits equally well but selects worse. The supplement gives bootstrap
intervals but no parameter correlations.\\
\action Report parameter correlations and an identifiability analysis per GPU from the
existing calibration data, and compare fitted terms with the hardware-counter data so that
the parameters have a physical reading. Optional: calibration keys that discriminate the
two forms.

\finding{M3}{The reach of the model is described inconsistently}
\evidence Limitations says transfer to another kernel is untested, but
Table~\texttt{tab:reference} reports an instance for the CUDA scan with exchange terms
(4.9\%, fitted on 60 timings), and the pipelines have a work-term model.\\
\action State exactly what was transferred: the eight-parameter form belongs to the Triton
scan; other kernels needed different terms; no form was carried over unchanged.

\finding{M4}{The central claim has no model behind it}
\evidence The paper explains the second-order result in prose. An explicit bound follows
from its own data: the step gain cannot exceed the scan share times the scan regret.
Latency-weighted scan shares times the default's latency-weighted regret on the RTX~3090
give 0.43, 0.21 and 0.05\%, above the measured best gains of 0.29/0.19, 0.01/0.01 and
0.03/0.00\%, although the two inputs use different call mixes and boards.\\
\action Add this compositional bound as a stated result, recomputed on one mix and
validated per shape, so that a step-level effect can be predicted from single-call data.
Test a bottleneck model of launch and device time against the heuristic launch floor.

\subsection*{Measurement}

\finding{E1}{The power attribution is observational}
\evidence Value effects are attributed to switching activity under the power cap from
throttle readings and one citation; clocks could not be locked.\\
\action Label the attribution as consistent with the throttle readings, not as
established, unless an intervention on clocks or power limit can be run.

\finding{E2}{Data sets with weaker protocols feed the results}
\evidence The cloud instances may be virtualized, and some data sets lack the exclusive-GPU
guard.\\
\action Mark the provenance of each headline number and say which headlines depend on an
unguarded data set.

\finding{E3}{Profiling-cost figures do not reconcile}
\evidence The Cost paragraph gives 3.9, 7.4 and 6.1\,s for 57 keys, which is 68--130\,ms per
key, next to ``30 to 50\,ms of timing per key'', which matches supplementary medians over a
different set of 33 keys. The stated times give a payback of 1888--2480 keys, while the text
says ``about 1600 to 2500''.\\
\action Trace these figures to the result files and name the key set and statistic for each.

\subsection*{Presentation}

\finding{P1}{Revision history leaks into the paper}
\evidence ``of this version'' (Platforms; Timing protocol, twice), ``the earlier
launch-inclusive sweep'' (Results), ``the earlier data sets'' (Limitations), ``first sweep''
(supplement).\\
\action Name each measurement campaign by its protocol and remove every reference to versions.

\finding{P2}{Parts of the Results are too dense to review}
\evidence The paragraph ``Chunk versus launch configuration'' contains about 60 numeric
tokens.\\
\action Move these values to a table and keep one claim per paragraph. Treat the Accuracy
paragraph the same way.

\finding{P3}{Inconsistent terms}
\evidence Table~\texttt{tab:baselines} says ``six a priori protocols'' while the leading rule
was fixed afterwards. ``Headroom'' is both the estimand of Eq.~\texttt{eq:headroom} and a
held-out regret in the Glossary.\\
\action Drop ``a priori'' for the post-hoc rule and separate the estimand from its estimator.
