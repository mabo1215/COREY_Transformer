# Reviewer 1 (Methodology) -- IEEE TPDS regular paper

**Manuscript:** "Shape Sets the Chunk: A Compile-Informed Cost Model and a No-Gain Bound for Data-Dependent Dispatch of Chunked Linear-Recurrence Kernels" (main body `paper/main.tex`, 14 pp.; Supplementary Material `paper/appendix.tex`, 16 pp.; reviewed jointly).

**Reviewer persona:** GPU performance measurement, analytical/empirical performance modelling, statistics for systems papers.
**Independence:** this review was written from the manuscript sources, the reviewer rule files, `src/experiments/tpds/README.md` and the file names under `src/experiments/results/tpds/` only. No prior review, progress file or result content was consulted.

## 2. Recommendation: **Major Revision**

The numeric aggregate sits at the lower end of the minor-revision band, but the decisive reasons for a major revision are that (i) several headline numbers (0.9% regret, 0.45 us headroom, 94% tied on the RTX PRO 6000) are reported in a timing convention in which, by the authors' own account, 64% of the captured calls are launch-bound, and the device-time replication that fixes this exists for the RTX 3090 and H800 but not for the headline GPU; (ii) the per-call oracle that every regret, tie share and headroom is measured against comes from a single timing block, while the supplement shows that two re-timings of the identical kernel differ "significantly" in 68-74% of pairs; (iii) the abstract's "below one host synchronization at every tuned shape" claim for the 1.4B/2.8B checkpoints is contradicted by the only tabulated per-shape counts (Table S-headroom-noise still lists 1/1 and 7/5 shapes above one synchronization, flagged as superseded, with no replacement entry); and (iv) the cost-model form was selected on the captured calls that serve as its headline test set, which the abstract does not qualify. Each required measurement is cheap (minutes to about an hour of GPU time), and the direction of every conclusion is likely to survive; but the headline numbers must be re-derived, so this is not a text-only revision.

## 3. Findings

Effort classes: (a) text only; (b) offline analysis of existing result files; (c) new GPU measurement (estimated GPU-hours).

1. **[CRITICAL] Headline GPU results are launch-inclusive, where most calls are launch-bound.** Evidence: Sec. 5.4 "Timing protocol": "a call shorter than its host-side launch cost (56 to 90 us) is timed together with the wait for its launch: these launch-inclusive latencies, used unless stated otherwise"; Supp. Sec. "Timing Conventions": "46% (64%) of the captured 370M calls have an optimal latency below 100 us; ... below it they differ by up to a factor of about seven, and the per-call oracle differs for about a quarter of the calls." On the RTX 3090, switching to device time moves the static regret from 17.7% to 32.9%, the shape tree from 1.9% to 0.4% and the headroom from 2.16 to 0.03 us (Table S-conventions). The abstract, Table 3 (predictors), Table 7 (replication: "Held-out headroom 0.45") and Sec. 6.3 all quote RTX PRO 6000 numbers in the launch-inclusive convention only; the result listing contains `_r3spin` device-time files for `h800` and `vgpu3090` but none for `pro6000`. Action: re-time the 2360 captured Mamba-1 calls (and the calibration sweep) on the RTX PRO 6000 in device time, refit the model, and add a PRO 6000 row pair to Table S-conventions; report headline regrets/headroom in both conventions or state the convention in the abstract. Effort: (c), about 0.5 GPU-hour (2360 calls x 6 chunks x 60 repeats plus the 2298-timing sweep).

2. **[CRITICAL] The per-call oracle is estimated from one timing block although between-block noise is documented to be large.** Evidence: Supp. "Trace capture": "Each captured call is timed at every chunk with 10 warmup runs and 50 timed repeats; its per-call oracle is the fastest chunk" (no rounds, no order randomisation mentioned, in contrast to the value-invariance and TP sweeps where "chunk and input order are shuffled in every round"); Supp. "Oracle Headroom and Timing Noise": "two such timings of the identical kernel differ by a median of 2.0 to 2.2% (90th percentile about 30%), 68 to 74% of the pairs differ significantly". A single-block argmin over six chunks is therefore biased toward whichever chunk caught a fast block, which inflates p95 regrets (e.g., 135.5% in Table 3, 383.9% in Table 2) and deflates tie shares in a way the split-half analysis (within-block only) cannot repair. Action: time every captured call in at least three rounds with the chunk order shuffled per call and define the per-call oracle on the round medians; report the resulting Tables 2-3 and the headroom. Effort: (c), about 0.3 GPU-hour per GPU per checkpoint (the `_r3` files for the 1.4B/2.8B runs suggest the harness already supports this; apply it to the 370M PRO 6000 and RTX 3090 sweeps).

3. **[MAJOR] The abstract's "below one host synchronization at every tuned shape" claim for 1.4B/2.8B is not supported by any tabulated per-shape count, and the only table entries say the opposite.** Evidence: Abstract: "For the Mamba-1 scan up to 2.8B parameters that headroom stays below one host synchronization at every tuned shape on held-out prompts"; Sec. 6.4: "the held-out headroom at tuned shapes is 0.18 and 0.32 us per call, at most 4.7 us at any tuned shape" (the 4.7 us figure appears nowhere in the supplement); Table S-headroom-noise rows "M1 1.4B, 3090*: Above sync 1 / 1" and "M1 2.8B, 3090*: 7 / 5", footnoted as superseded by the re-timing, but the re-timed data are reported only as means (Table S-conventions, "Headroom 0.18 / 0.32" and "2.59 / 4.80" launch-inclusive). The launch-inclusive per-shape maximum for the re-timed 2.8B calls is never given, and the comparison of a device-time headroom with a launch-inclusive synchronization cost mixes conventions (inside a CUDA graph a host-side decision is impossible anyway, so the relevant decision cost there is the device-side reduction, 7.3-42 us). Action: replace the two superseded rows with the re-timed rows (in-sample, held-out, split-half, above-sync in both conventions) and state in the abstract which convention and which decision cost the claim refers to. Effort: (b).

4. **[MAJOR] Model-form selection used the headline test set; the abstract and conclusion report the in-sample-form numbers without qualification.** Evidence: Sec. 4 "Model form and data": "The terms of Eq. (4) were chosen with an ablation that scored candidate terms on the captured calls ... The captured calls are therefore a clean test set for the fitted parameters, which never see them, but not for the choice of terms"; Table S-model-ablation shows regret on the captured calls varying from 0.87% to 8.45% (PRO 6000) and 3.24% to 17.19% (RTX 3090) across the twelve candidate forms, i.e., the selection had material leverage on the RTX 3090 number. The clean tests are the H800 (3.5%), the width transfers (3.7%, 0.8%) and the TP shards (6.5%). Action: either (i) lead the abstract/conclusion with the form-blind numbers (H800, 1.4B/2.8B, shard widths) and label 0.9%/3.2% as "form selected on these calls", or (ii) re-select the form on the calibration sweep plus leave-one-key-out only (both are available offline) and report the captured-call regret of that form. Effort: (b).

5. **[MAJOR] "Tied" is defined by overlapping 95% CIs from one 50-repeat block, which the paper's own noise data show is not a calibrated test of equality.** Evidence: Sec. 5.4: "We call two kernel-level configurations tied when their 95% confidence intervals overlap. Within one session this criterion is conservative"; Supp. headroom-noise: within-block CV is 3.7-4.7% launch-inclusive and about 0.5% in device time, while between-block noise makes 68-74% of identical re-timings "differ significantly". Overlapping-CI is a liberal criterion for declaring ties (non-overlap at 95% corresponds to roughly p < 0.006), while the too-narrow within-block CIs push the other way; the net calibration of the 90-100% tie shares in Tables 1, 3, 4, 7 is unknown. Action: report tie shares under a practical-equivalence margin (chunk within 2% and 5% of the oracle, as already done for TOST) alongside, or replace, the CI-overlap definition; if CI overlap is kept, compute the CI from the between-round variance of finding 2. Effort: (b) once finding 2's rounds exist, (b) for the margin-based version now.

6. **[MAJOR] The "activation statistics add nothing" test is weak by construction: a depth-3 tree with at most eight leaves and no uncertainty on the regret.** Evidence: Sec. 6.3: "Each predictor is a depth-3 decision tree ... The decisive comparison is between the shape and the shape plus activation statistics: ... adding entropy or all activation moments to the shape leaves the regret at 0.7%"; Table 3 reports regrets without confidence intervals; the exact-shape floor is 0.36% (Table S-model-params), so the shape tree itself leaves 0.35 points on the table that a richer model could attribute to entropy or to shape. The mutual-information check conditions on the length bucket, not the exact shape key. Action: (i) add prompt-grouped bootstrap CIs to every regret column of Tables 3 and 4 and state an equivalence margin for "adds nothing"; (ii) stratify by the exact shape key and test, within strata, whether entropy (or any moment) predicts the per-call oracle (e.g., permutation test of the held-out regret of an exact-shape table augmented by an entropy split); (iii) report the conditional MI given the exact key. Effort: (b).

7. **[MAJOR] "Regret"/"headroom" is used in three incompatible senses, and the same policy on the same calls is quoted as 23.4% and 10.6%.** Evidence: Table 3 caption: "Regret is mean / 95th percentile in percent" (per-call mean of relative regret; best single chunk 23.4% on Mamba-1, RTX PRO 6000); Sec. 6.8: "Over the 2360 captured Mamba-1 calls on the RTX PRO 6000 it is 34 us per call (10.6%) against a single profiled chunk" (aggregate microseconds divided by aggregate latency); Table 4 and Table 7 then switch to "us/call". A reader cannot tell which quantity Proposition 1 bounds, and the per-call relative mean is dominated by sub-100 us launch-bound calls (finding 1). Action: add a definitions paragraph to Sec. 5.4 with the three estimators written out (per-call relative mean, latency-weighted share, absolute per-call mean), say which one each table uses, and use the latency-weighted or absolute form wherever the result feeds Eq. (7)-(8). Effort: (a) plus (b) to recompute one consistent column where needed.

8. **[MAJOR] All kernel-level sweeps are warm-L2, which the paper shows produces up to 43% placement effects at L = 8192; the oracle chunk under a cold L2 is never reported.** Evidence: Sec. 5.4: "Repeats run back to back without flushing the L2 cache, so the latencies are those of a warm cache"; Supp. "Value invariance: residual input effects": "With a warm L2 the placements differ by 29 to 41% at chunks 32 to 256 ...; with a cold L2 the same placements differ by 0.5 to 2.3%"; and "In serving, where other kernels run between two scans of a layer, the warm-L2 advantage of a favorable placement is not guaranteed either." Table S-l2 reports per-chunk ranges only, not whether the argmin or the tuned chunk changes between warm and cold. Since the cost model has no L2 term ("with L2-miss term" changes nothing in Table S-model-ablation), a cold-L2 regime could change the best chunk at long lengths and the model would not follow. Action: re-time the twelve tuning keys at the six chunks (and, if cheap, the captured calls at L >= 2048) with the L2 overwritten before every repeat on the RTX PRO 6000; report whether the per-key best chunk, the cost-model selection and the headroom change. Effort: (c), about 0.5-1 GPU-hour.

9. **[MAJOR] The RTX 3090 serving-replay conclusion for budget batching rests on one measured key with 11 captured calls.** Evidence: Supp. "Key Churn in Serving Traces": "the RTX 3090 leaves 90.8% of the calls below the host-synchronization break-even in one hour of the Azure code trace ... most of these calls map to one measured key (bucket 2048, batch 4, unaligned) that has only 11 captured calls and a bucket-level headroom above one synchronization, so its break-even is unbounded"; main Sec. 6.8: "keys that include the batch of a budget step multiply, to 13 to 852, and leave up to 42 and 93%". The 93% figure is therefore a sampling artifact of the capture design, not a property of the workload, yet it is carried into the main text. Action: capture or synthesise (the kernels are value-invariant) at least 50 calls of the unaligned bucket-2048 batch-4 key on the RTX 3090, re-estimate h_k, and re-run the replay; also report, per trace row, how many calls map to keys with fewer than 30 captured calls. Effort: (c), under 0.2 GPU-hour, then (b).

10. **[MINOR] Routed end-to-end evidence is batch 1 only, where the kernel-level static regret is 1.7-5%.** Evidence: Sec. 6.6: "on prompts of 256, 976 and 4096 tokens with 32 greedy tokens"; "At 256 and 976 tokens the prefill takes about 46 ms whatever the chunk". Table 2 shows the chunk matters most at batch 4 and 16 (13-24% static regret), and Table S-overhead shows that at L = 8192, batch 16 the stride-8 histogram costs 10.9 ms against an 8.5 ms scan, so the conclusion would almost certainly survive, but it is not demonstrated end to end. Action: add one batched configuration (e.g., batch 8 x 2048 tokens) to the routed study with the static chunks, the stock dispatch, the sampled histogram and the moment proxy. Effort: (c), about 1-2 GPU-hours on the RTX 3090 (30 rounds).

Action: reconcile each pair, state the data set each R^2 refers to, add the missing values to the supplement tables. Effort: (a)/(b).

12. **[MINOR] The per-shape maxima quoted as held-out are the in-sample column.** Evidence: Sec. 6.8: "Keyed on the exact length and batch size and chosen on held-out prompt folds, the table leaves 0.45, 2.2 and 2.2 us per call ... and at most 4.4, 6.7 and 14.5 us at any tuned shape"; Table S-headroom-noise caption: "In-sample: table chosen on the same calls (largest shape in parentheses)" with entries "0.34 (4.4)", "1.86 (6.7)", "2.07 (14.5)". The held-out per-shape maximum is not tabulated; only the "Above sync 0 / 0" count supports the "every tuned shape" claim. Action: add a held-out per-shape maximum column and quote it. Effort: (b).

## 5. Strengths

- The measurement pitfall of split-call chunk timing is identified and avoided; every kernel-level number comes from a recurrence-preserving launch with an output check.
- The noise analysis is far above the norm for systems papers: BH-corrected two-way F tests with effect sizes, TOST equivalence bounds, independent redraws, a value-vs-placement decomposition on two GPUs, an L2 cold/warm intervention, split-half and null headroom estimates, and an explicit statement that the headroom is an upper bound.
- The cost model is validated the right way in several directions (leave-one-key-out, parameter transfer between GPUs, width transfer to 1.4B/2.8B without refitting, TP shard widths, H800 with eight refitted parameters) and comes with a term-by-term ablation and physically plausible parameters.
- The routed study is a single-session, 30-round, randomly interleaved, within-round paired design with explicit "statistic computed but not routed" controls that isolate the decision cost.
- Limitations are stated candidly (form selection on the captured calls, non-bandwidth-optimal histogram, batch-1-only device-side cost, synthetic TP inputs, simulated serving).
