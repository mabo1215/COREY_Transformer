Dear Editor-in-Chief,

We are pleased to submit our manuscript entitled "Shape Sets the Chunk: A Compile-Informed Cost Model and a No-Gain Bound for Data-Dependent Dispatch of Chunked Linear-Recurrence Kernels" for consideration as a regular paper in the IEEE Transactions on Parallel and Distributed Systems (TPDS).

The chunked linear-recurrence kernels behind Mamba, Mamba-2 and Gated DeltaNet expose a chunk size that runtimes tune offline and cache per shape. In parallel deployments this scales poorly: continuous batching and chunked prefill make the shapes of kernel calls follow the request stream, tensor and sequence parallelism change the per-device shapes, and mixed GPU fleets multiply the keys by the device types. The manuscript examines two ways around per-shape tuning: predicting the chunk from shape and hardware, and choosing it per call from a statistic of the activations.

The main contributions of the manuscript are:

1. A compile-informed cost model of the chunked scan that takes the register allocation, spills and thread layout of the compiled kernel and eight parameters calibrated once per GPU. Without timing any target shape, it selects the chunk of 2360 captured prefill calls with 0.9% and 3.2% mean regret on two GPU generations, well below a table built from the same calibration data, and transfers across GPUs and model widths.

2. A no-gain bound for data-dependent dispatch: a policy that reads the activations can beat a shape-keyed table only where the table's oracle headroom exceeds the decision's critical-path cost. We instantiate it with value-invariance experiments, predictor studies (activation statistics add nothing to the shape), held-out and noise-corrected headroom estimates, and a single-session routed end-to-end study.

3. An analysis of tuning caches under key churn, including a replay of public LLM serving traces (Azure LLM inference traces and BurstGPT) under continuous batching and chunked prefill, which shows how the choice of shape key decides whether any online policy can pay off.

4. Measurement guidance for chunked kernels, including a documented pitfall, split-call chunk timing, that does not measure a chunked scan.

We believe the manuscript is well suited to TPDS because it concerns performance modeling and auto-tuning of GPU kernels, runtime dispatch inside the inference critical path, and the tuning-cache design that parallel and heterogeneous serving deployments require. The data and code are available through a Code Ocean capsule.

Relation to an earlier version. While preparing this manuscript, we found that the kernel-level chunk results of an earlier, unpublished version of this study had been obtained with a split-call timing harness, which cuts the sequence into independent kernel calls, resets the recurrent state and mostly measures the number of kernel launches. We withdrew all such results, replaced them with measurements that change the chunk inside a single recurrence-preserving launch on two GPU generations, added real-trace, predictor, auto-tuning and break-even experiments, and reframed the paper around a predictive cost model and a bound for data-dependent dispatch. To preserve double-anonymous review, the manuscript describes the measurement pitfall in general terms and does not refer to the earlier version.

This manuscript is original, has not been published previously, and is not under consideration for publication elsewhere. All authors have approved the submission. To the best of our knowledge, there are no conflicts of interest that would affect the review process. In compliance with the double-anonymous review policy of TPDS, the submitted manuscript and the Supplementary Material are anonymized, and the code and data are provided for review at https://codeocean.com/capsule/3457679/tree. The use of AI tools (Grammarly for grammar checking; Claude for debugging experiment code, writing and debugging parts of the measurement and analysis scripts, and cross-checking reported numbers) is disclosed in the Acknowledgment section of the manuscript, following the IEEE policy on AI-generated content.
