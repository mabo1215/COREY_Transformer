\section*{Scope and Inputs}

This file opens revision cycle tpds-r3. It is a fresh, independent review
of the current manuscript (main body, 14 pages, and supplement) written
from the paper alone, without reference to earlier review rounds, against
the standard of the IEEE Transactions on Parallel and Distributed Systems
(regular paper, double-anonymous, 14 formatted pages). The author opened
the cycle on 2026-09-30. Items that need new GPU experiments are listed
with an effort estimate; they are run only after the author selects them.

Effort classes: (a) text only; (b) offline analysis of existing result
files; (c) new GPU measurements.

\section*{Summary and Recommendation}

The paper asks whether a per-call activation statistic (the entropy-driven
COREY scheduler) can choose the chunk size of chunked linear-recurrence
GPU kernels better than shape-keyed tuning. It argues from
data-obliviousness that it cannot, states a no-gain bound (gain is at most
oracle headroom minus decision cost), and measures value invariance,
per-call oracles and predictors on captured Mamba-1 and Mamba-2 prefill
calls on two GPUs. It adds an eight-parameter cost model of the Mamba-1
scan built from compile facts that selects the chunk with 0.9\% and 3.2\%
mean regret without timing the target shapes. The measurement work is
careful and candid.

\textbf{Recommendation: major revision.} The work is sound but narrow:
the chunk matters materially only on the authors' own Triton kernel, the
theorem is close to a tautology whose numerical verdict depends on a
debatable minimum decision cost, the cost model has thin out-of-sample
evidence, there is no parallel or distributed dimension, and the paper is
still organized around refuting the authors' own scheduler.

\section*{Major Concerns}

\subsection*{M1. Venue fit: no parallel or distributed dimension}
Everything is one kernel on one GPU; batches stack equal-length prompts;
multi-GPU deployment gets one sentence (Scope of batching; Discussion;
Limitations). TPDS reviewers will ask why the paper is not at an ML or
kernel venue.
\begin{enumerate}[label=M1\alph*.]
  \item (a) Present the zero-shot cross-device transfer of the cost model
        as a heterogeneous-fleet result, and discuss tensor, sequence and
        context parallelism: tensor parallelism shards the channel width
        (per-device width $d/t$), sequence or context parallelism shards
        the length (per-device length $L/p$), and both change the grid,
        wave and occupancy terms of the cost model.
  \item (b) Replay public request-length traces of LLM serving (for
        example the Azure LLM inference traces and BurstGPT) under
        continuous batching, chunked prefill and variable-length packing;
        count distinct shape keys and how often each recurs; apply the
        key-churn break-even with the measured per-key call counts and the
        cost model as the default for unseen keys. This turns the
        break-even section into a systems result.
  \item (c, 2--4 GPU-h, single GPU) Tensor-parallel width study: predict
        the chunk offline for per-device widths $d/2$, $d/4$, $d/8$ of the
        1.4B and 2.8B checkpoints, then time them on one GPU.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{M2. Practical stakes: the chunk matters mainly for the authors' kernel}
Mamba-2 SSD is flat (every policy within 2.4\% of the oracle), Gated
DeltaNet is capped at chunk 64 which is always optimal, and the 8.5$\times$
spread comes from a Triton kernel written for the study that is
1.23$\times$ slower than the reference scan (geometric mean) and whose
chunk curve is driven by register spills. The reference-scan sweep helps,
but it confounds chunk with thread count.
\begin{enumerate}[label=M2\alph*.]
  \item (a) State the thread-count confound of the reference-scan sweep;
        scope headline claims to what is measured (drop ``three kernel
        families'' from the headline unless Gated DeltaNet is measured on
        real traces and above chunk 64).
  \item (c, about 1 GPU-day) Push Mamba-2 SSD and Gated DeltaNet out of the
        launch floor (2.7B-class widths, batch 16, 8K tokens) and capture
        real Gated DeltaNet traces from a public checkpoint.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{M3. Theorem 1 is near-tautological and its bite depends on $o_{\min}$}
The proof follows in one line from the definition of the headroom. The
minimum decision cost is defined over any statistic that depends on the
activations, which includes statistics fused into a preceding kernel,
overlapped on another stream, or evaluated by a device-side conditional
graph node; these can cost less than the measured 7.3\,$\mu$s. For the
1.4B and 2.8B checkpoints the in-sample headroom (10.7 and 86\,$\mu$s)
exceeds one host synchronization and no held-out value is given.
\begin{enumerate}[label=M3\alph*.]
  \item (a) Rename the result a proposition; define $o$ as the
        critical-path increment; state which policy classes the numerical
        verdict excludes (host-side and separate-kernel decisions) and
        which it does not (fused, overlapped, conditional-node decisions);
        make the information evidence (shape plus entropy equals shape) the
        primary argument.
  \item (b) Estimate the headroom with an independent-repeat oracle (choose
        the argmin on one half of the repeats, score on the other), or a
        parametric bootstrap from the stored per-chunk mean and standard
        deviation, so that noise-driven headroom is separated from real
        headroom.
  \item (b) Report held-out headroom for the 1.4B and 2.8B checkpoints.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{M4. Cost-model validation}
The fit uses timings that contain no captured call and leave-one-key-out
gives 1.0\% / 2.0\%. However: the model form was chosen with ablations
scored on the captured calls; the effective sample is about twelve keys;
$R^2$ on log latency is dominated by the work term and says little about
the ranking of chunks within one shape; the reference-scan model is a
re-specification; and the RTX 3090 tied share (69\%) and p95 regret
(15.4\%) are not in the main text. The nearest-tuned-key table built from
the same calibration data is a missing baseline.
\begin{enumerate}[label=M4\alph*.]
  \item (b) Add ``nearest calibrated key'' and ``work-only model'' rows to
        the predictor table; report within-shape rank agreement (Kendall
        $\tau$), p95 regret and tied share on both GPUs.
  \item (a) Declare the model form frozen and state which data chose it;
        describe the reference-scan variant as an instance of the same
        methodology.
  \item (c, about 0.5 GPU-day) Test the frozen form on a third,
        data-centre GPU (A100, H100 or H800), fitting only the per-GPU
        parameters.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{M5. The framing still reads as a refutation of the authors' own method}
The keywords include ``negative results''; a full section describes
COREY; the cost model is the sixth of seven contribution bullets; the
routed table mostly shows that a naive histogram with a host
synchronization is slow, which depends on the implementation, yet
``24 to 193\%'' is in the abstract; and no external work proposes
activation-driven chunk choice, so readers will ask why the question
matters.
\begin{enumerate}[label=M5\alph*.]
  \item (a) Reposition the paper around the cost model, the tuning-cache
        and key-churn analysis, and a general bound for data-dependent
        dispatch; present COREY as one instance of input-aware variant
        selection and cite that line of work (input-aware autotuning,
        algorithmic choice, sparse-format selection).
  \item (a) Drop ``negative results'' from the keywords; shorten the COREY
        section; move the full routed table to the supplement and keep a
        few representative rows in the main text.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{M6. Measurement rigor}
Value invariance uses three rounds per cell and TOST at 2\% is at the
resolution limit; the 43\% fixed-chunk effects on the RTX PRO 6000 are
attributed to buffer placement, but the placement control ran only on the
RTX 3090; ``tied'' uses within-session confidence intervals; the protocol
does not state clock, power, thermal or L2 policy (the per-call sweeps run
with a warm L2); the 2.8B sweep ran under heavy host load; the 48\,GB RTX
3090 board and its isolation are not described; some legacy routed results
rely on a build or per-run records that were not retained.
\begin{enumerate}[label=M6\alph*.]
  \item (a) Document clocks, L2 state, isolation and the 48\,GB board in
        the timing protocol; state the tie criterion's limits and pair it
        with a practical margin; remove from the evidence base results
        whose code or per-run records were not retained, or mark them
        clearly as legacy.
  \item (c, about 3 GPU-h) Placement control plus hardware counters on the
        RTX PRO 6000.
  \item (c, 4--6 GPU-h) Re-run the 2.8B sweep on an unloaded host and add
        batch 4 and 16 for the 1.4B and 2.8B checkpoints.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{M7. Density and readability}
The abstract has about fifteen numbers, uses COREY without defining it,
contains a ``2.1 points'' without a referent and ends on a dangling
sentence; the value-invariance subsection packs about forty numbers into
seventy-five lines; the break-even discussion is hard to parse.
\begin{enumerate}[label=M7\alph*.]
  \item (a) Rewrite the abstract with at most five numbers.
  \item (a) Summarize value invariance in one table and move the ANOVA,
        redraw and placement details to the supplement; compress the
        break-even discussion.
  \item (a) Merge Proposition 1 and the no-gain result; give the cost
        model its own section.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{Minor Issues}
\begin{enumerate}[label=N\arabic*.]
  \item Title: 21 words and centred on activation-driven scheduling;
        shorten and centre it on the cost model and data-dependent
        dispatch.
  \item Abstract wording: name the GPUs next to paired numbers; say
        ``without timing the target shapes'' rather than ``without
        profiling'' (the model needs a one-time calibration).
  \item The contrast ``shape-keyed 0.7\% versus entropy 21\%'' is not
        like-for-like; add a batch-only predictor row and lead with
        ``shape plus entropy equals shape''.
  \item Proposition 1: ``shape'' must include every compiler
        specialization (divisibility, strides, dtype).
  \item Table I: explain cells with two optimal chunks; note that on
        Mamba-2 the regret is measurement noise (maximum regret exceeds
        the chunk spread).
  \item Fix awkward sentences in the cost-model subsection and relieve
        cramped floats (column separation of Table IV, Table V, tiny
        labels in Fig.~1).
  \item Fig.~6 plots call-weighted means while the text quotes medians;
        make them consistent.
  \item Remove legacy RTX 3070 / H800 numbers from the main text.
  \item State where the break-even and churn results are interpreted that
        prompt lengths were truncated and rotated by design, so the length
        distribution is synthetic.
  \item Reduce output preservation to one sentence (it holds by
        construction).
  \item Delete the supplement section on prospective analyses and the
        main-text pointer to it.
  \item References: update venues of arXiv entries that have since been
        published; add GPU performance modelling, measurement bias,
        input-aware tuning, LLM serving, and 2025--2026 linear-recurrence
        kernel and model work.
  \item Verify that the code and data availability link is anonymous for
        double-anonymous review.
\end{enumerate}
